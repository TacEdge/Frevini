01 — Foundation

One system.
Kember’s own story.

Kember Interiors shares a design system with Frevini Studio and nothing else. The master brand must hold floor, wall, ceiling, joinery and components; its positioning is being defined separately, and this guide leaves room for it.

Established by Kember
  • “Where Craftsmanship Meets Nature. American Northern Oak for Every Room.”
  • American Northern Oak from certified forests.
  • Three-ply, all-hardwood engineered construction: 4 mm wear layer, 11 mm core, 4 mm balancing layer.
  • Hardwax oil finish. Lifetime warranty, subject to maintenance.
  • Family-owned, vertically integrated.
Transferred from Frevini
  • Typography — Marcellus and DM Sans, the same hierarchy and sizes.
  • Layout — 12-column A4 and 16:9 grids, margins, spacing scale, footer logic.
  • Restraint — one brand colour, square corners, horizontal rules only, no boxes or shadows.
  • Documents — the cover, page, slide and poster archetypes.
  • Voice — short statement, then a sentence of proof.
Kember’s own
  • Name and lock-up — KEMBER INTERIORS. The wordmark is retained; “kreative interiors” is retired for New Zealand.
  • Colour — Kember Umber, drawn from the deep oak fields already used by Kember.
  • Material — American oak, in Kember’s own photography.
  • Engineering — the three-layer section as an information device.
  • Products — Flooring, Wall + Ceiling, wider capability, labelled by availability.
  • Working expression — “American oak. Engineered for stability.” Provisional until the positioning work is complete.

One mark, one colour.

Logo

Wordmark reconstructed from kemberfloors.com captures · the official master governs

The lock-up: the retained KEMBER wordmark with INTERIORS in DM Sans Medium, +0.22 em, right-aligned to the wordmark. Clear space = half the wordmark height on all sides.

Reversed on Kember Umber or over darkened photography.

  • Minimum width 30 mm print · 130 px screen (wordmark alone 22 mm · 96 px)
  • Cover width 44 mm on A4 · 210 px on a slide
  • Position top-left at the margin; bottom-left on posters
  • Appears on covers, title and closing slides, posters, product sheets. Interior pages carry the running footer.
  • Colour Kember Umber or white only. The grey and blue of the current mark are not used in New Zealand material.

04 — Photography and graphic language

Oak does the work.

Frevini’s photographic discipline applied to Kember’s subjects: the space, the oak, the construction, the making, the application.

American oak, live sawn
Architecture

The finished space with the oak floor as the subject; natural light, level horizontals, joinery as scale.

Text on imagery

White Marcellus over a black overlay at 30–45 % or a bottom-up gradient, bottom-left at the margin.

Not this

The blue-graded, render-like hero imagery on the current site: cool cast, artificial light, no material.

Material

Northern oak as it arrives: log ends, bark, sawn character. Tight crop, one plane in focus.

Engineering

The section device stands in where no photograph of the construction exists.

Making

The mill and log yard, documentary and unstaged. Overcast light is welcome; no grading.

Application

Floor, wall and ceiling in use. One application per image; the material continuous.

Principles
  • Natural, directional light; warm-neutral balance; restrained saturation. Colour from the oak, not the grade.
  • Level horizontals; one quiet edge for type; square corners; full bleed or on column edges.
  • One hero per page; support images at column width; one application per image; never a collage.
Do not use
  • Blue grading, HDR, filters, brown-tinted overlays, generic luxury-house imagery, stock lifestyle photography.
  • Rounded image cards, circular buttons, quote-mark glyphs and photo-filled display type as on the current site.
  • Renders presented as photographs; collages; drop shadows; decorative shapes.

Images on this page are reference captures from kemberfloors.com; the log yard is the one Kember-made photograph among them and sets the standard for the commission. A Kember photography commission in the five categories, at 300 ppi for print, is required before production collateral.

07 — Voice and governance

The Kember test.

Voice

Short, exact, unhurried. A statement of three to six words, then a sentence of measurable proof. American facts in New Zealand English: metric, sentence case, no exclamation marks, no “transform your living space”.

DoDon’t
American oak. Engineered for stability.Transform your living spaces with exceptional quality!
Three plies, all hardwood. A 4 mm wear layer over an 11 mm two-directional core.Superior strength and durability for modern living.
Installed like a 19 mm solid floor. Less seasonal gapping.Your floors stay gorgeous for years.
Hardwax oil finish. Lifetime warranty, maintained.Industry-leading, all-wood, lifetime peace of mind.
Request samples →VIEW MORE →
Principles
  • Say it short. Specific beats superlative. Design and engineering in one breath.
  • Assured, not salesy. New Zealand English, metric first. Address the specifier as a peer.
  • Say what Kember says. Where Kember publishes no figure, say nothing.
Before issue

Any “no” sends it back. Then hide the lock-up: is it still Kember, and not Frevini?

  1. 1  Does it use the approved typography hierarchy: Marcellus sets it, DM Sans carries it, Medium for emphasis?
  2. 2  Is whitespace structural — about forty percent open in editorial and presentation layouts, generous on technical pages?
  3. 3  Is the palette umber, Oak White and white, with Slate at most once and no blue?
  4. 4  Is oak, in photograph or section, doing the visual work?
  5. 5  Is the hierarchy immediate: one eyebrow, one heading, one idea?
  6. 6  Have boxes, shadows, gradients, rounded cards and circular buttons been removed?
  7. 7  Is the language specific, with a number where a claim is made?
  8. 8  Is technical content set with the same care as marketing content, and labelled available or capability?
  9. 9  Does it meet the Frevini standard without being mistaken for Frevini?
  10. 10  With the lock-up hidden, is it still a serious architectural material brand?
