01 — Foundation

Built, not branded.

One dark green does all the work. One editorial serif carries every heading, one contemporary sans carries everything else, and neither is set bold in the standard hierarchy. Pages are square-cornered, left-aligned and built on open field; photography supplies the warmth.

Established
  • “Built not Branded.”
  • “Where design intent meets technical mastery.”
  • “Better. Bespoke. European Made.”
  • Made in Šiauliai, Lithuania. Studio in Wellington.
  • Written for specifiers: architects, designers, installers.
Observed
  • Architectural — hard rectangles, full bleed, no rounded corners.
  • Restrained — one colour for text, buttons, fields and rules.
  • Editorial — large serif against small sans, wide margins.
  • Material — every hero image is grain, bark, leaf or board.
  • Credible — 15/4 mm, WBP birch ply, FSC, 25-year warranty.
Extended for documents
  • Calm density — about forty percent open field in editorial and presentation layouts; technical pages may be denser.
  • Serif as signature — Marcellus sets the hierarchy of every document; technical continuation pages may run in DM Sans alone.
  • One accent per page — green is the accent; photography is the colour.

Logo

  • Never recoloured, outlined, shadowed, stretched, rotated, watermarked, or shown twice on one face.

04 — Imagery

Material first.

Photography supplies the colour and warmth the palette withholds. Four kinds of image, each with a job to do.

Deep Creek, herringbone
Architecture

Oblique floor view, warm directional light. The floor is the subject; furniture gives scale. Covers, openers, full-image pages.

Text on imagery

White Marcellus over a black overlay at 30–45 % or a bottom-up gradient, bottom-left at the margin. Never over faces, grain detail or the horizon.

Detail

Grain, bark, leaf, junction. Tight crop, shallow depth, one plane in focus. Text + image pages, quotes, closing pages.

Material and process

Raw material, tactile, documentary. Logs, stacked boards, finishing, samples. Narrative and technical documents.

Sample grid

The one permitted use of cut-outs on white: swatches on a strict grid, square-cornered, evenly spaced, no shadows.

Light and colour
  • Natural, directional, soft light; late-afternoon warmth is the reference.
  • Warm-neutral white balance, restrained saturation. Colour from the subject, not the grade.
  • No HDR, filters, vignettes or coloured overlays.
Composition and crop
  • Level horizontals; oblique floor views encouraged; one quiet edge for type.
  • Square corners; full bleed or on column edges; no bordered insets, no masks.
  • One hero image per page; support images at column width; never a collage.
Do not use
  • Stock lifestyle photography, or renders posing as photographs.
  • Posed people or people looking to camera; people only working with the material.
  • Wide-angle or drone distortion, cluttered showrooms, unidentified floors, watermarks.

Voice

Frevini writes the way it builds: short, exact, unhurried. A three-to-six-word statement, then a sentence of proof.

  1. 1  Say it short. “Built not Branded.” “Why oak?” Full stops are allowed when the line is a statement.
  2. 2  Specific beats superlative. 25-year warranty, 15/4 mm, FSC, Šiauliai. Never “premium” or “world-class”.
  3. 3  Design and technical in one breath. Intent and proof in the same paragraph.
  4. 4  Assured, not salesy. No exclamation marks, no urgency. “Connect with us to explore how we can support your next project.”
  5. 5  Plain New Zealand English, European precision. Sentence case, “we”, metric, en-dashes for ranges.
  6. 6  Address the specifier as a peer. “A collaborative partner for specifiers.”

07 — Governance

The Frevini test.

Before anything is issued, answer each question. Any “no” sends it back. Then hide the logo: is it still Frevini?

  1. 1  Is the typography unmistakably Frevini — Marcellus setting the hierarchy, DM Sans for the rest, Medium for emphasis?
  2. 2  Is whitespace structural — about forty percent open in editorial and presentation layouts, generous margins and grouping on technical pages?
  3. 3  Is the palette green, warm white and white — Olive at most once, no other hue outside photography?
  4. 4  Is every image doing work — material, detail or a finished space — with one hero at most?
  5. 5  Is the hierarchy immediate: one eyebrow, one heading, one idea, horizontal rules only?
  6. 6  Have boxes, shadows, gradients, rounded corners and icon-bullets been removed?
  7. 7  Does every spread pair a statement with a proof?
  8. 8  Would it look at home beside frevini.co.nz?
